# Feline Performance, Interaction, and Sound

These are animation direction choices for Fia & Theo, not veterinary claims or a guarantee that a model follows instructions. Preserve the chosen stylization and latest approved episode rules.

## Feline acting and emotion

Use gaze, ears, head angle, body distance, pauses, whiskers, tail motion and weight shifts. Do not default to human palms, pointing, shoulder shrugs, clenched fists, human lip movements, embraces, or sustained two-legged acting. An explicitly approved anthropomorphic special episode may change a specific behavior; it must not rewrite the series baseline.

Choose a visible trigger and response, rather than only naming an emotion. Optional acting proposals:

| Story emotion | Visible trigger and readable response |
| --- | --- |
| Jealousy or hurt | The observer sees a rival's contact, interrupts a step, watches briefly, then turns away. |
| Protective tension | Theo notices a threat, steps into its approach line, lowers his body and directs his gaze toward it. |
| Surprise | A nearby event interrupts movement, ears orient and a brief recoil precedes recovery. |
| Curiosity | Fia orients toward a new object, approaches to sniff and tests it with one paw. |
| Embarrassment | After a visible mistake, the cat pauses and checks the other cat's reaction. |
| Reconciliation | One cat approaches slowly; the other chooses to stay; a brief cheek rub resolves the distance. |

Use one or two useful cues, not every cue at once. A tail flick or ear angle alone must not be described as proof of a real cat's emotion. Keep expressive animation readable without turning faces into human masks.

## Quadruped movement

Maintain four limbs and one connected tail, sensible support and weight transfer. Select walk, trot, gallop, crouch, pounce, or scramble appropriate to the beat; do not prescribe a walking footfall pattern for every running speed. Fur and pendants follow movement without changing attachment or design.

A jump has preparation, push-off, travel, contact, and recovery. Airborne frames are valid inside a jump. A slip is an intentional temporary loss of traction, not an error to ban globally. Define the slick surface and affected foot, preserve momentum, limit unintended body rotation, then show a plausible recovery. After contact is restored, avoid continued frictionless sliding.

For turns, show foot placement and body lean before changing travel direction. For ledges or stairs, establish usable surfaces and landing space. Do not invent a global jump-height limit; respect the current style, scale and approved fantasy allowance.

## One actor and one responder at contact

Plan each contact with initiator, target, body part, approach, contact, reaction, release, and end state. Keep a short beat focused on one interaction. Do not ask both cats to lick, turn, step and embrace simultaneously.

Grooming example: Theo brings his muzzle toward Fia's forehead, makes one brief tongue contact, withdraws; Fia stays balanced, then slowly closes her eyes. The actor's tongue remains attached to the actor. No merging muzzles, swapped faces, extended human-like kissing or intersecting heads. Include a reason for grooming when the story depends on it, such as a visible leaf or a voluntary affectionate approach. Do not invent that reason after rendering.

Cheek rubbing example: one cat closes the distance, their cheeks briefly contact, heads slide past gently, then separate. Keep legs independently supported and pendants distinct. A second response follows after the first contact reads.

Collision example: show the approach and actual hip or shoulder contact before the receiver recoils. Contact may be a bump, block, miss or deflection; a miss must not cause a hit reaction. Do not impose a universal 0.3-0.8 second reaction delay. The immediate physical response follows the event; a later realization may use a comic pause.

Doorway gag: show the available opening and the body region meeting it, compress fur modestly within the style, and preserve anatomy. No magically narrowing frame, detached legs, body passing through wood or unexplained teleport out. The release must follow an identifiable movement or another character's help.

## Prop ownership and release

For fish, bread, keys, toys and loose accessories record holder, grasp site, orientation, approximate scale, support, release event, trajectory and next owner. While gripped in a mouth, a prop follows that muzzle, not an unrelated cat or camera. After release it responds to gravity/contact or a specifically approved magical force. Do not let food hover or follow a fleeing cat automatically.

For a transfer, keep one object: giver holds, receiver contacts, giver releases, receiver carries. A shared tug is permitted only when both grips and the object between them stay visible. During occlusion preserve ownership and identity; do not swap the cats when they reappear. A fish cannot duplicate because the camera passes behind a tail.

## Camera for feline contact

Apply `action-framing.md` as contact-led attention: initiator -> approach -> touch/release -> response. A medium three-quarter view often gives enough room for both cats; use a close-up only when it keeps the event legible. A locked-camera request remains locked.

Avoid orbiting, sudden zooms or changing camera side during a difficult groom, slip or catch. Keep enough of the initiating cat, contact region and responding body visible. Effects and motion blur must not cover the essential evidence. Use landmarks to show intentional turns rather than prescribing an unchanging screen-left direction.

## Cat vocals and event-based audio

The opted-in series default is instrumental music plus feline reactions and environmental Foley. An explicit no-music, silent, or sound-only request overrides that default. Preserve supplied music and exact approved cues unless changes are requested.

Use short context-appropriate feline meows, chirps, trills, hisses, purrs or restrained growls; identify which cat makes the sound and what causes it. Do not use the phrase 'gasp meow' or ambiguous human reaction spellings. No human speech, human gasp, scream, laughter, grunts, narration, singing, choir or backing vocals unless explicitly requested for a special episode. Do not replace a cat with a lion roar or default toy squeak.

A mouth holding a fish cannot simultaneously perform a wide open-mouth meow while maintaining the same grip. Place that vocal after visible release, or choose a compatible closed-mouth cue. Avoid making every cat vocalize at once. A bell sounds from an actual movement; it need not ring on every frame. Purring and Foley should not blanket the whole track.

Build a compact event map: visible cause -> sound source -> onset -> duration/decay -> music change. Examples: paw loses traction -> wet skid; released fish contacts floor -> soft wet contact; Theo sees the loss after releasing it -> one short feline reaction. Distinguish physical sound onset from the later emotional reaction.

## Music and pauses

Match the episode: lightly tense playful music for jealousy, a rising instrumental rhythm for a chase, brief softening for reconciliation. Do not use a calm romance bed under frantic comedy by default, nor continuous maximum loudness.

Use a short music reduction around the central mistake or reveal when it helps the joke. Keep ambience and important Foley unless silence itself is intended. Choose pauses from the actual action, not a mandatory number of dropouts or a fixed 10-15 second silent block. Keep the musical phrase coherent and leave an audible decay for the ending.

If native audio is unsupported, separate the audio plan without pretending it will be generated. Do not substitute a speech engine for animal sounds or sound effects. Do not assert that a waveform proves a vocal sounds feline; perceptual audio review is a separate evidence gate.
