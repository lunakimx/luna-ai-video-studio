## Seven directing checks

1. **Situation before biography.** Start with a visible present-tense problem, desire, imbalance or anomaly. Preserve the approved hook and actual first-frame state. A still tableau may carry tension; do not invent a collision just to satisfy a timer.
2. **First-frame relationship.** Make placement, accessible space, wardrobe, relevant props and lighting support the same immediate situation. Do not redesign locked clothing or add props to manufacture symbolism. The opening may withhold an identity or motive intentionally; make the immediate question legible.
3. **Meaningful distance.** Specify who can approach, reach, block, observe or escape, and the visible change in that relationship. Close does not always mean affection; context and posture distinguish intimacy from threat. Use landmarks or body lengths when useful, not arbitrary precise measurements. Respect the path and contact limits.
4. **Directed gaze and knowledge.** Name observer, target, line of sight and the moment the target becomes perceptible. Show recognition before the resulting action. If a character learns through sound or an established off-screen event, declare that cue; do not invent an unseen source. Distinguish what the audience knows from what each character knows. Never expose hidden eyes through the back of a head.
5. **Functional props.** Give each narratively emphasized prop a job: goal, obstacle, clue, trigger or payoff. Track initial location/owner, grip or attachment, release, path and settled state when it moves. Background decoration need not have a payoff. Plant the needed evidence before a prop-dependent outcome; do not duplicate a dropped object or add unapproved magic.
6. **Causal action order.** Track available cue -> perception -> choice/action -> contact or miss -> response -> changed state. Not every beat needs each step shown separately; every necessary dependency must remain inferable. Preserve intentional dramatic irony, delayed recognition and user-approved nonlinear order. A character must not react to knowledge they do not yet possess.
7. **Caption-hidden review.** Temporarily ignore explanatory captions and narration during review. Can the viewer identify the immediate desire/problem, the consequential action and the changed situation or relationship? If not, repair order, framing, action size or recognition time before adding explanation. This is a diagnostic pass, not permission to delete requested captions, dialogue, music or sound-led storytelling. Assess a dialogue-dependent joke's exact verbal payoff with audio as a separate pass.

## Coverage and continuity

- Select each view for information: cue, observation, action, consequence, reaction or reveal. Single-character coverage can establish a relationship through matched eyelines, consistent off-screen positions and a stable room landmark. A two-shot is optional; honor an explicit ban. Use a two-shot when permitted and necessary to understand contact or relative scale.
- A continuous take can transfer attention by blocking, a modest pan/reframe or a motivated focus change. Do not silently insert reverse-angle cuts, teleport the camera or claim independent generations are one take.
- Preserve one clear primary event at a time. Supporting business is useful only if it helps the current joke or story and does not conceal the cue or payoff.
- For separate generations, carry a compact shared look specification: source direction, soft/hard quality, exposure intent, white-balance intent, palette, skin/fur rendering and contrast. Use an approved look reference only in an actually supported input role. Inspect real boundaries; identical wording cannot guarantee matching color.
- Allow enough on-screen time for cue recognition, the action and its consequence. Do not impose a universal reaction delay, camera-change interval or joke count.

## Evidence and repair

Use PASS, REVISE, UNVERIFIED or N/A with the relevant beat or actual inspected timestamp. A written cue proves an instruction exists, not that the model performed it. A still cannot verify a gaze shift, release order or reaction delay. Uninspected audio stays UNVERIFIED. Do not invent timestamps, viewer studies or view-count predictions.

Repair the smallest cause: missing cue -> unreadable staging/eyeline -> action order -> prop state -> timing -> camera complexity. Apply only the authorized changes and preserve successful parts. For a deliberate verbal or off-screen reveal, retain the intended device and fix contradictory visual evidence instead of demanding that every fact be shown.

Before delivery, run the seven checks at the applicable evidence level and the project's existing QC. If only the prompt was assessed, report prompt-level validation only. Generated-video quality and viral performance require actual output evidence.
